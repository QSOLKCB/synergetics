# Mapping Rules

SYNERGETICS is a comparative research instrument. Its purpose is to locate useful shared structure without erasing differences between source systems.

## Relation classes

| Class | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| `EXACT` | A declared reproducible transformation preserves the relevant structure. Rare by design. |
| `STRUCTURAL` | A meaningful invariant, relation or organisation is preserved. |
| `FUNCTIONAL` | Different structures perform comparable roles in their own systems. |
| `ANALOGICAL` | The relation aids interpretation but does not establish mathematical or ontological identity. |
| `HYPOTHETICAL` | A proposed bridge awaiting formalisation or testing. |
| `REJECTED` | The comparison was examined and found misleading or unsupported. |

## Evidence codes

- `P` — primary or recognised source material
- `R` — reproducible repository implementation
- `D` — derived relation or mathematical analysis
- `E` — empirical observation or assessment
- `H` — interpretive hypothesis

Classification and evidence are independent. A primary source can support an analogy without making it an exact mapping.

## Shared-signal rule

Non-rejected cross-station mappings pass through a `shared.*` parameter. This prevents the lab from silently asserting direct equivalence between, for example:

- vector equilibrium and the Buddhist Middle Way;
- the Threefold Training and ternary protocol states;
- Fano incidence and the E8 root system;
- ecological reciprocity and mechanical equilibrium.

Direct cross-station relations are permitted only when their classification is `REJECTED`, so the failed comparison remains inspectable.

## Field score

For each selected station parameter and candidate shared signal, the lab combines:

1. relation-class weight;
2. shared domain overlap;
3. evidence support.

The field score combines the mean station score, weakest station score and station coverage. The weakest station matters deliberately: one excellent correspondence cannot conceal four weak analogies.

The score is a navigation and comparison aid. It is not a probability of truth, scientific validity, spiritual correctness or historical influence.

## Residual

The residual is derived from the weakest station match. It represents meaning not captured by the shared signal, including differences in:

- units;
- dimensions;
- ontology;
- historical purpose;
- normative commitments;
- physical versus computational interpretation;
- spiritual versus engineering goals.

A high residual is useful information, not a software failure.

## Sonification

The field sonification is a receiver projection:

- each station receives one voice;
- stronger matches are more consonant and stable;
- weaker matches receive greater detuning and reduced spectral clarity;
- the centre signal receives a quiet reference tone.

Audio does not provide evidence for a mapping. It communicates the registry's declared score.
